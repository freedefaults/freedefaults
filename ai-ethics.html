AI Ethics

Identify and discuss ethical issues associated with AI and ML and their societal implications.

One of the biggest ethical issues I see with AI and machine learning is that a system can work exactly how it was designed and still cause harm. From my own experience using AI, I've noticed that these systems can be very accurate with familiar situations but struggle when something unexpected comes up. I've experimented with image recognition technology and noticed that even small changes in an object's position or appearance can cause the system to make mistakes. This becomes a much bigger concern when AI is being used to make decisions that affect people's lives. If a system struggles to recognize something as simple as an object, how much should we trust it to make decisions about someone's future?

Another concern I have is how AI is being used in the hiring process. As a finance student who has gone through multiple internship and co-op applications, I know how competitive finding a position can be. The idea that an automated system could reject a qualified applicant before a real person even reviews their resume is concerning. Something as simple as an employment gap could negatively affect someone's chances even if they have a perfectly reasonable explanation for it. I understand why companies use AI to make hiring more efficient, but efficiency shouldn't come at the expense of fairness. How is someone supposed to have an equal opportunity if the system screening them is already working against them?

I've also noticed how easy it is to become overly reliant on AI, especially when using tools like ChatGPT or Gemini. I've personally used both for questions and research, and while they can provide helpful information, I've also seen them give answers that aren't entirely accurate. The issue is that these answers are often presented with enough confidence that someone might not think to question them. This becomes especially dangerous when people turn to AI for medical advice or other serious situations. Just because a system gives the correct answer a few times doesn't mean it will always be reliable. I think people need to recognize that AI should be used as a resource rather than something they blindly trust.

Voice cloning is another example of how AI can be used for both good and bad purposes. As someone who works with music and technology through DJing and music production, I can see how useful AI-generated audio can be creatively. At the same time, the ability to recreate someone's voice creates opportunities for scams and impersonation. Someone could realistically receive a phone call that sounds like a family member asking for money without realizing the voice is fake. I believe responsibility falls on both the people using AI and the companies creating it. Developers should have safeguards like consent verification and human review of suspicious activity rather than leaving people to deal with the consequences themselves. AI has the potential to be extremely beneficial, but that doesn't mean we should ignore the risks that come with it.
