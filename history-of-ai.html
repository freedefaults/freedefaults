History of AI

One of the first key moments in AI was in 1950 when Alan Turing proposed testing whether a machine could hold a conversation without someone realizing it was a machine. This connects directly to chatbots today and how realistic their responses can sound. In 1956, the Dartmouth Workshop helped establish AI as a field, with early systems relying on rules written by humans. However, expectations grew faster than the technology could keep up, leading to funding cuts and the first AI winter in the 1970s. Expert systems brought interest back in the 1980s by using specialized knowledge for specific tasks, but they were expensive to maintain and struggled outside their programmed rules. How could these systems handle new situations if someone had to write a rule for each one? During the 1990s, machine learning became more prominent by allowing systems to learn patterns from data. Larger datasets and stronger computing power eventually led to AlexNet’s image recognition breakthrough in 2012. Transformers followed in 2017 and helped AI process relationships within language, laying the groundwork for systems like ChatGPT. Modern AI can now learn from examples and generate content, although producing a convincing answer still does not guarantee that it is correct.
