History of AI

One of the first key moments in the development of artificial intelligence was in 1950 when Alan Turing proposed testing whether a machine could hold a conversation without someone realizing they were speaking to a computer. Looking at how AI works today, I think this idea was way ahead of its time. I've personally used tools like ChatGPT and Gemini for research, schoolwork, and everyday questions, and sometimes the responses can sound almost identical to something a real person would say. In 1956, the Dartmouth Workshop helped establish AI as its own field of research. Early systems were mostly built around rules that humans had to program themselves. However, expectations for AI grew much faster than the technology could actually develop, eventually leading to funding cuts and what became known as the first AI winter in the 1970s.

During the 1980s, AI started gaining attention again through expert systems, which were designed to use specialized knowledge to solve specific problems. These systems were useful in certain situations but they were expensive to maintain and struggled when something happened outside of their programmed rules. How could a system handle new situations if someone had to manually write a rule for every possible outcome? I think this is one of the biggest differences between early AI and what we have today. From my own experience working with Excel and financial data during my co-ops, I understand how useful automation can be, but also how limited something becomes when it can only follow a specific set of instructions. If the information changes or something unexpected comes up, the system needs to be able to adapt rather than just follow the same process every time.

During the 1990s, machine learning became more prominent because researchers were developing systems that could recognize patterns from data rather than relying entirely on instructions written by humans. As computing power improved and larger datasets became available, AI was able to accomplish things that weren't realistic decades earlier. One major breakthrough came in 2012 when AlexNet demonstrated how effective deep learning could be for image recognition. Another major development happened in 2017 with the introduction of transformers, which helped AI better understand relationships between words and process language more effectively. This eventually laid the groundwork for modern systems like ChatGPT. I find this interesting because AI went from struggling with basic programmed rules to being able to generate entire conversations, analyze information, and even assist with creative projects.

Today, I use AI for a variety of things that probably wouldn't have been possible with earlier systems. Whether I'm using it to help understand financial concepts, organize information, or experiment with ideas for music production, it's impressive how much the technology has developed. At the same time, I've noticed that just because AI produces a convincing response doesn't necessarily mean it's correct. I've had situations where ChatGPT gave me an answer that sounded completely reasonable but turned out to be inaccurate when I looked into it further. I think understanding the history of AI makes it easier to recognize both how far the technology has come and where its limitations still exist. AI has gone from following basic rules to learning patterns and generating original content, but human judgment is still extremely important when deciding how much we should trust it.
